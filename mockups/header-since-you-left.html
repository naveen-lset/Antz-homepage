Home header: “Since you left”

Last round copied Swiggy’s look. This round starts from the content: the header says what changed since you last opened the app, in one sentence, with the animals’ photos inside it. Everything else earns its place or goes (table below). Swiggy supplied four ideas, not the look: a photo inside the type, a rolling search hint, a band colour that means something, and tiles that are doors. The wild animal doodles are the wallpaper, and the lead story’s animal is drawn in large.

Headline
Each underlined name opens that exact record: an animal’s page, the day’s births in Natality, the transfer. Three stories at most. The rest are counted in the ledger.
Photos
Only animals the news is about get a photo. A photo in the header always means “this changed”.
Search
The hint rolls through the names in today’s headline, then a tag and an enclosure, so people learn what search can find. Tap opens Global Search. Scan opens the tag reader.
Ledger
Every count opens its module page for the same time window (Born → Natality, Losses → Mortality, Unwell → Medical). 24 h / 7 days changes the headline and the ledger together.
Site
Opens a sheet with the four sites. It is a pill, not tabs: people change site a few times a month, not every visit.
Doodles
Decoration only: hidden from screen readers and not tappable. The large one is the lead story’s animal, drawn in when the story changes. After a loss they stop moving.

3 · How the headline is written

The sentence is assembled from data by rules, so it stays honest on every day, including the bad ones. Try each rule with the State buttons at the top.

Grammar

[window], [story], [story], and [story].
  • A story is a photo, the name (the tappable part) and what happened.
  • Three stories at most. A 4th adds a small “+2 more”, and every count is in the ledger.
  • An animal with a name gets its name (Ravi). Otherwise the count and species (3 chital fawns).

Order by role

  1. Keeper: my animals unwell → births in my section → arrivals → my first task.
  2. Vet: new or worse cases → procedures today → overdue vaccines → necropsy.
  3. Curator: losses → births → transfers → census done.

After a loss

  • The loss is its own sentence, first. It is never joined with “and” to good news.
  • The band turns slate, the doodles stop moving, and the photo is grey with no pop.
  • Only people with access see it: the vet, the curator and that section’s keepers.

Nothing happened

  • “A quiet night.” plus the first task and the day’s total. The header is never empty, and it never makes up news.
  • A zero in the ledger is dimmed, not hidden, so the row keeps its shape.
