Five ways to show visitors what ANTZ does

Each direction starts from a different idea, not a different card layout. Figures are real, from the V5 anonymised dump (data “today” = 20 May 2026), except where a card says sample. The panel beside each one says what happens when a visitor taps.

A. Ask the zoo

Visitors don’t know module names; they know questions. The page is a set of questions a zoo director asks. Each answer is a live insight, and the module that produced it is named as the source, so the modules come second, as the reason the answer exists.

On tap

  1. Tap a question → the answer card swaps in place (no page change), with a chart and the module it came from.
  2. Tap Open Natality → the real module page we already built (#m/natality).
  3. Modules without a page → a sheet: 3 insights + “What this module does”.
  4. 60 s idle → the questions cycle by themselves, like a quiz show.

Why it works

  • Explains value in the visitor’s words. A good fit for a booth where a salesperson talks over it.

Risk

  • Covers only as many modules as we write questions for (about 12).

On tap

  1. Tap a moment (outlined) → that module opens already filtered to this animal, e.g. Medical with its 4 vaccinations.
  2. Tap Meet another → a new animal, a new story, different modules lit.
  3. Below the story: a strip of every module, with the ones this animal touched highlighted.
  4. Idle → swaps to the next animal every 20 s.

Why it works

  • The most memorable one. People remember an animal, not a dashboard.

Risk

  • Few animals in the dump touch 4+ modules. We hand-pick 4–6 hero animals.

On tap

  1. Drag the clock bar → scenes light up and dim; the current one gets a red ring.
  2. Tap a scene → its module opens on that day (Medical shows the 45 doses of 19 May).

Why it works

Risk

D. Zoo map with module lenses

Place is the organiser. One map holds the 4 sites; each module is a lens laid over it. Switching lens re-colours the same map, so visitors see that every module sees the same zoo.

On tap

  1. Tap a lens → bubbles resize and recolour; the caption under each site changes. Try it on the left.
  2. Tap a site → it zooms to sections and enclosures, with the same lens kept.
  3. Tap an enclosure → a sheet with its animals and the lens module’s records.

Why it works

  • Spatial and touchable, great on a big iPad. Shows multi-site, a real ANTZ strength.

Risk

  • Needs an illustrated map. The positions here are invented.

E. Live pulse wall

Activity is the organiser. A big screen shows records arriving as they happen. Each one is tagged with its module, and the module counters along the top tick as records land. It says “this is a living system” without anyone reading a chart.

On tap

  1. Nothing needs a tap; it runs on its own.
  2. Tap a record → that animal’s profile (opens direction B’s story).
  3. Tap a counter → that module’s page, filtered to today.

Why it works

  • Draws people from across the hall. Pairs well with A or B on the iPad at the stand.

Risk

  • Mortality appears in a public feed. We could leave losses out of the wall.

My suggestion

  • B on the iPad at the stand. It’s the one visitors remember, and it shows modules working together.
  • E on the big screen behind it, to pull people in.
  • A is the safest single choice if there is only one screen.
